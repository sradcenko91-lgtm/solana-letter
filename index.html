A letter explaining what "Solana" actually is

August 17, 2026
Dear Mom and Dad,

You've heard me say "Solana" a dozen times now and I can tell you're just nodding along, so let me actually explain it properly — no jargon, promise. And when jargon sneaks in anyway, I'll cross it out and write what I actually mean right next to it, like this:

what is it, really
A blockchain → shared notebook is basically a notebook that keeps track of who owns what. Normally, one company keeps that notebook locked in a drawer — a bank, say — and you just have to trust them to write it down correctly and never lose it. Solana flips that. Instead of one drawer, there are thousands of identical copies of the same notebook, sitting on computers all over the world. Every time someone wants to add a new entry, all those computers check it and agree it's correct before it becomes permanent. Nobody can quietly erase a page or fudge a number, because everyone else's copy would disagree.

why anyone built this
The first notebooks like this — Bitcoin came first, in 2009, then Ethereum in 2015 — proved the idea actually works. But they're slow and get expensive once a lot of people use them at the same time, kind of like a small-town post office trying to sort every package in the country by hand. Solana launched in 2020 → built to handle the crowd was built specifically to not fall over under that kind of load — same idea, shared notebook nobody owns, just engineered to process thousands of entries every second, for a fraction of a cent each.

what people actually do with it
This is the part that made it click for me, so here's the honest comparison:

Doing thisThe old wayThe Solana way
Sending $200 to family in another country Bank wire: 1–3 days, ~$25–45 in fees About a second, less than a cent
Proving you own a ticket or collectible Only exists in one company's private database Lives in the shared notebook itself — no single company can delete it
Buying or selling something online Bank, app store, or reseller all take a cut in the middle The notebook itself handles the trade — fewer middlemen taking fees

That's really the appeal — not "mysterious internet money," but a notebook that's fast, cheap, and hard for any one party to rig. Turns out that's useful anytime two people want to exchange something and don't want, or can't afford, someone sitting in the middle.

So next time I mention "building something on Solana," now you know what I mean: I'm writing entries into a very fast, very cheap, shared notebook that nobody owns and thousands of computers keep honest.

Love you both,

Your kid
P.S. — two more words you'll hear
wallet → the app that holds your spot in the notebook, kind of like a bank app, except you're the bank.

token → one entry in the notebook that stands for something — money, a ticket, an item in a game, anything.
